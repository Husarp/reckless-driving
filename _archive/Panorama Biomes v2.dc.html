PANORAMA BIOMES  — extraction
Eleven biome verges, plus river and side-road crossings in a range of angles. The original four were lifted out of Departure Art with every lamppost and light pool removed. Nothing here draws street furniture. All plates are lane-count aware — change the lane count in Tweaks and everything redraws against the live road width.
Travel is bottom to top. A verge tiles forever on its own. A crossing is a fixed-height plate that marks a biome change: something wide passes under the road, each half carried by the verge on its side, so any biome joins any other with no per-pair art.
NEW BIOME VERGES  — BATCH 1
Five new verges in the existing idiom: same 140px plate, same primitives, same draw order, road painted last. Everything here is plan-view only — nothing needs sky, horizon or overhead space, so all of it ports to the game's 18px verge strip by cropping rather than re-authoring.
DESERT
Open hardpan on both verges: cracks, saguaro and scrub either side, and a dry wash on each — different phase, width and seed per side, so the two read as the same terrain rather than a mirror.
CANYON
The rock-walled version, now its own verge: warm rock rising both sides from the same terrain generator with its palette swapped and snow suppressed, scree fanning onto the shoulders.
INDUSTRIAL DISTRICT
Chain-link fence down both verges, an insulated pipe run parallel to travel, concrete pads. Tanks and stacks are drawn from above — a stack is a soot ring with a dark mouth, which is the only way it can exist in plan view.
QUARRY / MINING ROAD
Terraced benches stepping away from the road, rubble heaps, a conveyor running the length of the right verge. Dust bleeds over both shoulders, which is what separates it from Mountain at a glance.
MARSH / WETLAND
Standing water pooled by noise, reeds, tussocks and dead snags. Boardwalk planks replace the gravel shoulder — the one biome where the shoulder art itself changes.
VINEYARD / ORCHARD
Tilled rows run across the direction of travel; trees sit on a strict staggered grid. Depth is not faked with a horizon — regularity is what reads as "planted" from above.

CROSSING ANGLES  — VARIANTS
The original crossing had one fixed meander, which is what made the river look like it was wandering rather than passing under. The profile is now three numbers on one options object — straightness, angle and meander phase — evaluated identically by both halves, so any shape still joins as one continuous channel. Meadow both sides here so only the crossing changes. The underpass centre line follows the profile instead of running flat.
RIVER · SQUARE
Dead perpendicular. The calmest read, and the one that most clearly says "this passes under."
RIVER · ANGLED RIGHT
Straight but tilted 46px across the plate. Reads as a diagonal river with a skew bridge.
RIVER · ANGLED LEFT, SOFT BEND
Opposite tilt with the meander at half strength — a curve that still resolves under the deck.
RIVER · STRONG MEANDER
Meander pushed past the original with a shifted phase, so it does not repeat the same bend.
SIDE ROAD · SQUARE
A right-angle junction under the deck. The default I would ship.
SIDE ROAD · SKEW 52
The steepest angle that still keeps both kerbs inside the plate.
SIDE ROAD · SKEW −30, WIDE
Shallower tilt the other way on a wider deck — reads as a bigger road, not a lane.
SIDE ROAD · CURVED
A slight bend plus a slight tilt — a country road that is not surveyed straight.
EXISTING BIOME VERGES
Each one tiles vertically. Ground is painted first, scatter second, road last, so the carriageway is never overdrawn.
MEADOW
Flat green, flower scatter, occasional tree. The neutral default — safe to start a run in and safe to fall back to.
COAST
Sea and surf line on the left, dense forest on the right, sand blown onto the near shoulder. Asymmetric on purpose — it tells you which way you are facing.
MOUNTAIN
Rock rising on both sides with a lit/shaded slope, snow suppressed at this altitude, crash barrier and a cut face along the verge.
RIVER UNDER ROAD
A bend of water passing beneath, stone abutments where it disappears, low parapet on the road edge. Usable as its own verge or as the crossing filling.
CROSSINGS  — RIVER AND SIDE ROAD
Two fillings for one slot: water, or a side road running under yours. Both use the same profile and the same abutments, so the pair of biomes on either side is irrelevant.
RIVER  · MEADOW TO COAST

RIVER  · COAST TO MOUNTAIN

SIDE ROAD  · MOUNTAIN TO MEADOW

SIDE ROAD  · MEADOW TO MEADOW
