TANK — BARREL / TURRET JOINT
Built on the Reckless Vehicles pTank skin — same 24×32 cell, same olive, same tracks, stowage bins and louvred engine deck. Only the front of the hull, the gun and the turret row move. Zoom is 9× nearest-neighbour, true 1:1 underneath.
NOW — THE PROBLEM
1:1
The shaft is fillRect(11,2,2,9) — it ends at y=11, exactly where the turret's black outline starts. Abutting, never overlapping, so that outline reads as a cut across the gun.

Worse, the hull front is at y=2 as well, so no part of the barrel clears the hull. It's a dark line lying on the deck, and the 4×2 collar at y=8 becomes the thing your eye calls the barrel root.
A · LONG GUN
OLIVE
GREY
Hull front back to y=7, turret down to y=13, gun tip at y=1 — 9px of barrel in free air, 6px of it clear of the hull.

Shaft is drawn first and runs to y=18, under the turret. The old mid-barrel collar becomes a 6×2 mantlet straddling the turret face, and the shaft picks up a 1px black outline plus a 4px muzzle brake so it stays readable against traffic.

Catch: the mantlet is fixed grey metal, so on a grey paint job it sinks into the turret and the joint disappears.
A2 · PAINTED MANTLET
OLIVE
GREY
Identical to A in every pixel except the mantlet, which is now cast from the paint colour instead of grey metal: base shade(c,.02), lit top row shade(c,.34), and a 1px shade(c,-.3) edge on the right to match the turret's own shading.

It reads as an armoured casting bolted to the turret rather than a loose steel band, and it holds up on any paint — the joint is now defined by shading, not by a colour that only contrasts with olive.
B · LONGEST GUN
1:1
Same construction, pushed as far as the cell allows: hull front at y=10, turret at y=15, tip at y=0 — 12px of barrel, 10px clear of the hull.

Unmistakable as a gun at 1:1 and the joint can't be misread. Cost: the hull is down to 22px, so the tank loses some of its heavy, blocky mass next to the Road Train. Cell and hitbox unchanged in both.
DRAW-ORDER RULE
Barrel first, turret over it, mantlet last. Today the barrel is drawn after the turret, which is why it can never overlap — it has to stop short to avoid painting over the roof plate. Flipping the order is most of the fix; the mantlet is what sells it; the length is what makes it read at speed.
