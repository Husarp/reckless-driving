Menus, road art, energy bar
Everything below is drawn at the game's real 160×260 internal resolution and scaled up, so what you see is what the canvas can actually render. Road, verge and scenery are live-scrolling. Screens: main menu, road art before/after, in-game HUD, energy bar states, pause, leaderboard, run summary.
2a BIOME LOOP — LIVE
MEADOW
The verge is no longer random per slot. One 1920px cycle runs meadow → forest → farmland → suburbs → city → meadow, each biome with its own ground colour, prop table and density. Neighbouring biomes dither into each other over one slot so the change of ground reads as a transition, not a seam.

OVERPASSES
Two bridges per cycle, one in the forest and one in the city. The deck is drawn after the cars, so traffic genuinely disappears underneath for about half a second — a cast shadow band on the road tells you it is coming. Bridges also clear tall props from their approach so the deck never collides with a tower.
SET PIECES vs FILL
Trees, hedges, houses and towers are filled procedurally from the biome's weighted table. Signs, chevrons and roadside wrecks are hand-placed at fixed positions in the cycle, so they always land where they mean something: speed signs on entry, warnings before the forest, chevrons at the city approach.

SCENERY RULES
Verge is still 24px slots, but the slot table is 24 long instead of 14 and weighted: about 16 grass, 4 bush, 3 tree, 1 landmark. Grass slots are usually bare — a tuft only draws on roughly half of them, and never two tufts in adjacent slots. Landmarks (house, fence run, road sign) sit on one side only and never opposite each other, so the frame never feels walled in. Left and right use different tables so the two sides don't mirror.
